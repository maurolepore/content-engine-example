Voice Rules

These apply to everything — every script, hook, and caption. No exceptions.

The Seven Non-Negotiables

1. Short sentences
Maximum 15 words. If a sentence is running long, break it. Two short sentences beat one long one, every time.

2. Specific over general
"We saved a client three hours a day" beats "we helped a client become more efficient." Specificity is credibility. Generality is noise.

3. No AI-sounding language
Banned, no exceptions:
- "In today's fast-paced landscape"
- "It's a game-changer"
- "Excited to share"
- "Thrilled to announce"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Dive into"
- "Leverage" (as a verb)
- "Unlock"
- "Seamlessly"
- "Cutting-edge"
- "In conclusion"
- Any sentence that could've been written by a content agency for any founder anywhere

4. British confidence, not American hype
Direct. Slightly understated. The conviction comes from the clarity of the idea, not the volume of the delivery. Don't shout. Observe.

5. Claims need to be earned
If a script says something is true, it needs to prove it immediately. Don't state something is important and expect the audience to agree — show why. Specific example, real moment, concrete detail.

6. No waffle
Every sentence needs to be load-bearing. If removing a sentence doesn't change the meaning, remove it.

7. No performed vulnerability
Authentic honesty about difficulty is powerful. Performing struggle for engagement is immediately detectable and kills trust.
- Performance: "I was stressed."
- Real: "I sat there refreshing the inbox for an hour waiting for the client to reply, and it never came."

Hooks
Hooks get written last, once the real angle of the piece is clear — not first. A hook written before you know what you're actually saying ends up generic.

A hook should make someone with the problem immediately recognise themselves. "A few home truths about AI" tells the reader nothing. "Most businesses I talk to about AI have the same problem — they've already started in the wrong place" tells them exactly what they're getting.

Openers that tend to work (as a starting feel, not a template to fill in blindly):
- "Nobody talks about this..."
- "This will change how you see..."
- "I learned this too late..."
- "Most people don't realise..."
- "This is why you're still..."
- "Here's the truth about..."
- "Avoid this mistake when..."
- "If you're [specific situation]... listen carefully"

Rhythm
Mix short punchy fragments with longer explanatory sentences — don't smooth everything to the same length, the rhythm matters as much as the content. Slightly informal grammar (run-ons, comma splices) is fine if it reads like natural speech rather than written copy.

Platform format rules
LinkedIn:
- Lines 1 and 2 are the hook — written after the rest of the post, once you know what the real hook is
- Short paragraphs, 1 to 3 sentences maximum
- No external links in the post body — first comment only
- No more than 2 to 3 hashtags
- Never repost Instagram or TikTok content natively — the format doesn't translate and it visibly underperforms
- Best posting window: Tuesday and Wednesday, 10am-12pm
