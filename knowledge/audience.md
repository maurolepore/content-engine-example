Audience

Note: the demographic figures below are hypothetical estimates for the purposes of this build, not pulled from live analytics — reasonable planning assumptions, not verified numbers. Swap in real figures whenever convenient.

Who they are
Primarily founders, small and medium business owners, and AI-curious entrepreneurs. People actively running or building something, not passive tech enthusiasts. A smaller segment are aspiring AI agency owners looking to replicate the Briix model themselves.

Estimated demographics
- Gender split: approx. 70% male / 30% female
- Age range: concentrated 25-44, with the largest single band around 28-38
- Location: UK-heavy audience, with a meaningful US and wider English-speaking (Canada, Australia, Ireland) minority
- Platform breakdown: Instagram and TikTok drive the largest engagement. LinkedIn is smaller in volume but the warmest — audience there is in a business-decision mindset

Segments
1. The overwhelmed solo founder — running a business alone or with a tiny team, knows AI is relevant, doesn't know where to start, wary of being sold to
2. The SME operator — established small business, a handful of staff, interested in automating specific manual processes (lead follow-up, reporting, admin)
3. The aspiring AI agency owner — earlier in their journey, follows for the "how to build this yourself" angle, primary audience for the Agency Builder Program

What they care about
- Practical implementation over hype — "how do I actually use this in my business" not "isn't AI amazing"
- Time and cost saved, stated specifically (hours, £, headcount), not vaguely ("more efficient")
- Proof over promises — real client results, real numbers, real before/afters
- Honesty about difficulty — they trust admissions of what didn't work more than polished wins

What turns them off
- Generic AI hype language (see banned phrases in voice-rules.md)
- Feature-tour content that lists what a tool can do without showing a real outcome
- Anything that reads like it was written by a content agency rather than someone who actually does the work
- Performed struggle or manufactured vulnerability used purely for engagement

Topics that tend to resonate
- Real automation builds shown end to end, not just described
- "Before and after" transformations of a specific manual process
- Honest founder updates — what's working, what isn't, what changed this month
- Practical AI tool breakdowns aimed at non-technical business owners, not developers
